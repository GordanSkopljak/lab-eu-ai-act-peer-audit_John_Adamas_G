## Auditor prep notes

Not part of the record. Three things to hold under pressure.

1. **If John classified high risk, do not fold.** The reading that Annex III(4)(a) targets systems assessing candidates, not systems representing one, is stronger. Make him name the limb of 4(a) he thinks it falls under. If he cannot name it, the classification is a reflex rather than a determination. The closing argument is the consequence one: a reading under which a job seeker needs a conformity assessment to publish a chatbot about their own CV has taken a wrong turn.
2. **If John classified minimal risk, that is the weaker position.** Article 50(1) is why. Ask him what the bot says when a user types "am I talking to a real person".
3. **Finding 2 is the one that justifies the exercise, and it may be wrong.** It rests on one ambiguous phrase, "on the end user's behalf". If he answers that the recipient is hard coded, downgrade it openly in Step 2 and record the revision. Revising a finding on evidence is stronger auditing than defending one.

Timing point worth raising regardless of who is right on the tier: the Digital Omnibus deferred the Annex III obligations to 2 December 2027, while Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026. The action list is identical under both classifications, which makes the tier argument less consequential than it feels.
